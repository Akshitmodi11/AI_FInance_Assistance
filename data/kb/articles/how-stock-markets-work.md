# How Stock Markets Work

A stock market is where shares of public companies are bought and sold. Companies first sell
shares to raise money; after that, investors trade those shares with each other, and the
market price moves with supply and demand.

## Primary and secondary markets

When a company sells new shares to the public for the first time, in an **initial public
offering (IPO)**, the money goes to the company. That is the **primary market**. Almost all
everyday trading happens in the **secondary market**, where investors buy from and sell to
other investors; the company does not receive that money.

## Exchanges and brokers

Trades happen on exchanges such as the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq, and on other
electronic trading venues. Individual investors do not trade on an exchange directly; they
place orders through a **broker**, which routes them for execution.

## How prices are set

Each stock has a **bid** (the highest price a buyer is offering) and an **ask** (the lowest
price a seller will accept). A trade happens when they meet. Prices change constantly as news
about a company, the economy or interest rates changes what investors are willing to pay.

## Market and limit orders

A **market order** buys or sells right away at the best available price, which can differ from
the last price shown, especially in fast markets. A **limit order** sets the worst price you
will accept; it may not fill, but you control the price.

## Market hours

The major US exchanges are open for regular trading on weekdays from 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m.
Eastern time, apart from market holidays. Prices quoted outside those hours may be from the
previous close, which is why market data shows an "as of" time.
