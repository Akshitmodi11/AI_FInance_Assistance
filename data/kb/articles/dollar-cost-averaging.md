# Dollar-Cost Averaging

Dollar-cost averaging means investing a fixed amount on a regular schedule, for example $200
every month, no matter what the market is doing. It turns investing into a habit and removes
the pressure of deciding when the "right" moment is.

## How it works

Because the amount is fixed, you automatically buy **more shares when prices are low** and
**fewer when prices are high**. Over time this can lower the average cost per share compared
with buying the same number of shares each month.

## A short example

Suppose you invest $300 a month in a fund. In month one the price is $30, so you get 10 shares.
In month two it falls to $20, so you get 15 shares. In month three it is $30 again: 10 shares.
You invested $900 for 35 shares, an average cost of about $25.71, below the $26.67 average
price over those months.

## Benefits

- Builds a steady saving habit; most workplace retirement plans already work this way.
- Reduces the regret of putting everything in just before a drop.
- Takes emotion and market timing out of the decision.

## Limits

Dollar-cost averaging does not guarantee a profit or protect against losses in a falling
market. If you already have a lump sum, investing it all at once has historically come out
ahead more often than not, because markets have tended to rise over time; spreading it out
mainly reduces the risk of bad timing. Fees per purchase, if any, also matter.

## Pairing it with a plan

Setting the monthly amount from a budget, choosing diversified funds and automating the
transfer is a simple way to make progress towards a long-term goal.
