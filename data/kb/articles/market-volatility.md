# Market Volatility and Price Trends

Volatility describes how much and how quickly prices move up and down. A volatile stock can
jump or fall several percent in a day; a stable one moves less. Volatility is normal, and it is
the price investors pay for the higher long-run returns of stocks.

## How volatility is measured

The usual measure is the **standard deviation** of returns, often stated as **annualised
volatility**. A stock with 30% annualised volatility swings much more than a broad index with
15%. Another widely watched gauge is the VIX, which reflects expected volatility of the S&P 500
and tends to rise when markets fall.

## Moving averages

A **moving average** is the average closing price over a recent window, such as 20 or 50
trading days. It smooths out daily noise so the direction of the trend is easier to see. When
the price is above its 50-day average, the recent trend has been up; below it, down. When the
shorter 20-day average is above the 50-day average, the short-term trend is stronger than the
medium-term one.

## The 52-week range

The 52-week high and low show where a price has traded over the last year. A price near its
52-week high has been strong recently; one near its low has been weak. Neither tells you
whether the price will keep going.

## Living with volatility

- Daily moves are mostly noise for long-term goals.
- Diversified portfolios are usually less volatile than single stocks.
- Money needed soon belongs in less volatile holdings.
- Trend tools describe the past; they are not predictions or buy and sell signals.
