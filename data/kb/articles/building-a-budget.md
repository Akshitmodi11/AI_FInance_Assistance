# Building a Budget

A budget is a plan for your money: how much comes in each month and where it goes. It is not
about cutting everything you enjoy; it is about deciding on purpose, so savings and goals get
paid instead of whatever is left over at the end of the month.

## Step 1: Know your income and spending

Start with your take-home pay, the amount that actually reaches your account after taxes and
deductions. Then track spending for a month or two using bank statements or an app. Group it
into categories such as housing, food, transport, debt payments, subscriptions and fun.

## Step 2: Separate needs from wants

**Needs** are essentials: rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt
payments. **Wants** improve life but could be reduced: dining out, streaming services, travel.
Seeing the two side by side shows where there is room to free up money.

## The 50/30/20 guideline

A popular starting point splits take-home pay into about 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20%
for savings and extra debt repayment. It is a rule of thumb, not a requirement. In expensive
cities needs may take more than half; the useful part is making saving a fixed line in the
budget.

## Pay yourself first

Automate savings so money moves to savings or investment accounts on payday, before it can be
spent. Treating savings like a bill you must pay is one of the most reliable ways to reach a
goal.

## Review and adjust

A budget is a living plan. Check it monthly, compare plan to actual, and adjust categories when
income or costs change. Small leaks such as unused subscriptions are often the easiest wins.
