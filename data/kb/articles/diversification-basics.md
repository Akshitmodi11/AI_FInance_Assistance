# What Is Diversification?

Diversification means spreading your money across many different investments so that a
loss in one does not sink your whole portfolio. The idea is often summarized as "don't put
all your eggs in one basket."

## Why it helps

Different investments react differently to the same event. When one company has a bad
quarter, another may do well. Holding many of them smooths out the ups and downs, which
lowers the overall risk (volatility) of the portfolio without necessarily lowering the
long-run expected return by the same amount.

## Ways to diversify

- **Across asset classes:** stocks, bonds, and cash behave differently. Bonds often hold up
  better when stocks fall.
- **Within an asset class:** owning hundreds of companies instead of a few. A broad index
  fund does this in a single purchase.
- **Across sectors and countries:** technology, healthcare, energy; domestic and
  international markets.

## What diversification cannot do

It does not guarantee a profit or protect against loss in a market-wide decline, when most
investments fall together. It reduces the risk tied to any single company or sector, not
the risk of the market as a whole.

## A simple check

If one holding makes up more than about 20–25% of a portfolio, a large share of the
outcome depends on that single investment. Concentration is one of the first things a
portfolio review looks at.
