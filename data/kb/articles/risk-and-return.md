# Risk and Return

In investing, risk and return are linked. Investments that can earn more over time usually come
with bigger and more frequent swings in value, and a real chance of losing money. Investments
that are very stable usually pay less. There is no investment with high returns and no risk.

## What "risk" means

Risk is the uncertainty of what you will get back. It includes the chance of losing some or all
of your money, and the chance that results vary a lot from year to year. A common measure of
that variation is **volatility**: how widely an investment's returns move around their average.

## Common types of risk

- **Market risk:** the whole market falls, taking most investments down with it.
- **Company or business risk:** one company does badly, even when the market is fine.
- **Interest-rate risk:** when rates rise, existing bonds lose value.
- **Inflation risk:** returns fail to keep up with rising prices.
- **Liquidity risk:** you cannot sell quickly without accepting a lower price.

## The risk-return ladder

Roughly from lower to higher risk and expected return: insured savings accounts and money
market funds, short-term government bonds, longer-term and corporate bonds, large-company
stocks, then small-company and single-stock positions. Each step up offers a higher expected
return as payment for accepting more uncertainty.

## Matching risk to time

Money needed within a few years is usually kept in lower-risk places, because a market drop
right before you need it could be hard to recover from. Money for goals decades away can
usually take more risk, since there is time to ride out downturns. This is why the same person
can hold very different investments for different goals.
