# Managing and Paying Down Debt

Debt is not always bad: a mortgage or student loan can help build a future. But high-interest
debt, especially credit-card balances, compounds against you and makes every other goal harder.
Managing debt well is often the first step in a financial plan.

## Debt-to-income ratio

Your debt-to-income (DTI) ratio is your total monthly debt payments divided by your gross
monthly income. For example, $1,500 of monthly payments on $5,000 of gross income is a DTI of
30%. Lenders use it to judge whether you can take on more debt; a lower ratio means more room
in your budget.

## Avalanche vs snowball

Two popular ways to pay off several debts, both paying the minimum on everything first:

- **Avalanche:** put every extra dollar towards the debt with the **highest interest rate**.
  This saves the most money in interest.
- **Snowball:** put extra money towards the **smallest balance** first. Clearing a debt quickly
  feels motivating, which helps some people stick with the plan.

The avalanche is cheaper; the snowball can be easier to follow. The best method is the one you
will keep doing.

## Paying off debt vs investing

Paying off a debt is like earning a guaranteed return equal to its interest rate. Clearing a
credit card charging 22% beats most investments. Low-rate debt is less urgent, and many people
invest while paying it down, especially to capture an employer's retirement-plan match.

## Avoiding new debt

Paying cards in full each month, keeping an emergency fund, and budgeting for irregular costs
such as car maintenance help stop new balances from building up while old ones are paid off.
