# Understanding Your Risk Tolerance

Risk tolerance is how much ups and downs in your investments you can accept, both financially
and emotionally. It helps decide how much of your money goes into stocks versus safer holdings.
Investors are often described as conservative, moderate or aggressive.

## Ability vs willingness

Two different things make up risk tolerance. Your **ability** to take risk depends on your
situation: how long until you need the money, how stable your income is, and whether you have
an emergency fund. Your **willingness** is how you feel: whether a 25% drop would keep you up
at night or tempt you to sell. A good plan respects whichever of the two is lower.

## The three common profiles

- **Conservative:** stability comes first; mostly bonds and cash, smaller swings, lower expected
  growth.
- **Moderate:** a balance of stocks and bonds; meaningful growth with moderate declines.
- **Aggressive:** mostly stocks; the highest expected growth over long periods, with the largest
  and most frequent drops along the way.

## Time horizon changes the picture

Someone saving for retirement in 30 years can usually tolerate more short-term volatility than
someone buying a house next year, because there is time to recover from a downturn. Many people
become more conservative as a goal gets closer.

## Test yourself honestly

A useful question is what you did, or would do, in a sharp market decline. Selling after a big
drop locks in losses, so a portfolio you can actually stick with is often better than a riskier
one you would abandon. Risk tolerance can change with age, income and experience, so it is
worth revisiting from time to time.
