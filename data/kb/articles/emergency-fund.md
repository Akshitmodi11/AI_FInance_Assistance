# Building an Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is money set aside for unexpected expenses or a loss of income: a car repair,
a medical bill, or a job loss. Having it means a surprise does not have to go on a credit card
or force you to sell investments at a bad time.

## How much to save

A common guideline is three to six months of essential living expenses. People with less stable
income, a single income in the household, or dependants often aim for the higher end. If that
target feels far away, start smaller: even a few hundred dollars covers many common surprises,
and a first goal of one month of expenses is a solid milestone.

## Where to keep it

Emergency money should be **safe and easy to reach**, not invested for growth. Good homes are a
separate high-yield savings account or a money market account. Stocks are a poor fit because a
market drop and a personal emergency can happen at the same time, for example during a
recession.

## How to build it

- Set a monthly amount and automate the transfer on payday.
- Put windfalls such as tax refunds or bonuses towards it.
- Keep it in a separate account so it is not mixed with everyday spending.

## Emergency fund before investing

Many plans put a starter emergency fund ahead of investing, and alongside paying off
high-interest debt. Without one, the first unexpected bill can undo progress on every other
goal.

## Using it and refilling it

The fund exists to be used. When you spend from it for a real emergency, make rebuilding it a
priority in the budget until it is back to target.
